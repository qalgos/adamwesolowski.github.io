About

I’m a PhD student in computer science at Royal Holloway, University of London, supervised by Stephen Piddock, and currently a visiting student at the University of Oxford.

I work on quantum algorithms and quantum complexity: how quickly a quantum computer can answer questions about graphs, and how hard it really is for one to read the topology of data. Much of my recent work is on quantum topological data analysis, from estimating Betti numbers and detecting torsion to encoding simplicial complexes as quantum features, together with the hardness results that say when a quantum speedup can or cannot exist.

Before the PhD I read theoretical physics at Lancaster, and I spent three years in the quantum industry building machine learning methods for predicting properties of molecules. Away from research, I play chess and read French philosophy.

Research interests

  • Quantum topological data analysis

    The complexity of Betti number estimation, torsion detection in clique complexes, and quantum encodings of simplicial data.

  • Quantum graph algorithms

    Query complexity of shortest paths, replacement paths, eccentricity, radius and diameter.

  • Space, time and queries

    Trade-offs between the three in quantum graph algorithms, and most recently quantum catalytic logspace and its variants.

  • Quantum fine-grained complexity

    Conditional lower bounds that pin down exactly how much a quantum computer can help.
